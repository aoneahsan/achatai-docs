# Moving from TalkJS to AChat

**TalkJS is a pre-built chat UI to add to your product, free to use in development mode.** When chat belongs inside your own product and is tied to your own user accounts, TalkJS is the closer fit. AChat goes live on a free plan, and the visitors using it don't need accounts at all.

Checked 2026-11-07. The comparison page is [AChat and TalkJS](https://achat.aoneahsan.com/compare/talkjs).

## From development mode to a live room

TalkJS is free while you build in development mode. If what you prototyped was a public room rather than chat between your users, AChat can carry it live on its free plan.

1. Check what your prototype depends on. Conversations tied to your user IDs stay with TalkJS, because AChat's widget isn't a developer SDK and doesn't know your users.
2. For an open room, pick a room ID on the [widget page](https://achat.aoneahsan.com/widget) ([embeddable widget](/features/embeddable-widget)). Visitors join under a display name.
3. Pick **In the page** to sit where your TalkJS chat was, or **Chat button** for a corner panel.
4. Copy the code into the page.
5. Keep the room from an account, then list the websites allowed to show it.

## Compared

| Feature | AChat | TalkJS |
|---|---|---|
| Free plan with no end date | Yes | Partly (<a href="https://talkjs.com/pricing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">their page</a>) |
| Ready-made chat to add to a page | Yes | Yes (<a href="https://talkjs.com/pricing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">their page</a>) |
| Run it on your own servers | No | Not stated on the pages we checked |

The AChat widget shows an anonymous room or an open community channel. It isn't a developer SDK, and the room is readable by anyone on the page unless it has a password.
